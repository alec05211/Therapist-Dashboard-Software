# Owen Park session 1 The amount that kept changing
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. Not a clinical record or clinician-approved transcript.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager.
# Quiet, reserved delivery. Bracketed directions are not spoken. Six continuous parts; duration unmeasured.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: Before we start, what name would you like me to use?

OWEN: Owen's fine.

THERAPIST: What made this feel like the time to come in?

OWEN: My partner asked.

[pause]

THERAPIST: And what was it like for you to agree?

OWEN: Easier than another conversation about it.

THERAPIST: We can start there. What has that conversation been about?

OWEN: Drinking. Being home all day. Not doing much.

THERAPIST: Which part feels most possible to talk about today?

OWEN: Work, probably. I was a technical recruiter. They cut the team six weeks ago. I still get up at seven. There's just nowhere to put that.

THERAPIST: What happens after you get up?

OWEN: Coffee. Open the laptop. Move things between tabs.

THERAPIST: Applications?

OWEN: Sometimes. Mostly looking like applications.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: When does drinking enter the day?

OWEN: Evening. Couple beers.

THERAPIST: How many evenings in the last week?

OWEN: Three. Maybe four.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: Take your time.

OWEN: There was Sunday too. Five, I guess.

THERAPIST: You're revising that as you remember. What about the amount?

OWEN: Two. The tall cans. And sometimes a pour afterward.

THERAPIST: A pour of what?

OWEN: Whiskey. I haven't measured it.

THERAPIST: Then we don't yet have a reliable total. I don't want to turn two containers into two standard drinks without knowing more.

OWEN: I knew you were going to ask that.

THERAPIST: What did you imagine would happen when I did?

OWEN: You'd have a number. And then I'd be that number.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What would help you stay in this conversation?

OWEN: Don't tell me I'm brave for answering.

THERAPIST: All right.

[long pause]

OWEN: It's more nights than I said. Most nights since the layoff. I can't give you last Tuesday. I don't remember it clearly enough.

THERAPIST: Is that uncertainty about the amount, or are there parts of the evening you can't remember?

OWEN: Amount. I remember making dinner. I don't remember how much was left in the bottle before.

THERAPIST: I'll keep those separate. Have you had shaking, sweating, nausea, or felt unwell when drinking less?

OWEN: Shaky sometimes in the morning. I thought coffee. I don't know.

THERAPIST: Are you shaking or feeling unwell now?

OWEN: No. I ate before I came. My brother drove me.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: Have you ever had a seizure, severe confusion, or needed medical care when cutting down?

OWEN: No seizure. No hospital for that.

THERAPIST: Have alcohol and medications or other substances overlapped recently?

OWEN: An over-the-counter sleep thing. Once. I don't know the name.

THERAPIST: We need the name and timing, and a qualified medical assessment. I can't safely tell you to stop abruptly or work out a withdrawal plan here. Can we arrange a same-day medical call before you leave?

OWEN: You mean today.

THERAPIST: Yes. If you become acutely unwell, we need urgent medical care rather than waiting for therapy. Would you let me help with that call?

OWEN: If I don't have to explain it twice.

THERAPIST: We can ask what information they need and stay with the call together.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: You mentioned your brother drove. Have you driven after drinking recently?

OWEN: Once. Short trip.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: Did anyone get hurt?

OWEN: No. Nothing happened. I mean... I drove. That happened.

THERAPIST: Do you have access to the car tonight?

OWEN: Yes.

THERAPIST: Let's make a plan before you leave that doesn't depend on deciding correctly after drinking.

OWEN: My partner could hold the keys. My brother can take me home.

THERAPIST: Can we confirm both today?

OWEN: I can text him. I haven't asked my partner yet.

THERAPIST: We should record that as a plan to confirm, rather than something already in place.

OWEN: Okay. Please don't write that I'm sorted.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: How have you been feeling about being alive through this?

OWEN: Mostly tired. I want the day over. I don't want to die.

THERAPIST: Have you had thoughts of killing yourself or hurting yourself, made a plan, or taken steps toward it?

OWEN: No. None of that.

THERAPIST: If that changes, we'll need to talk directly and get support promptly. Who can you be with tonight?

OWEN: My brother until my partner gets home.

THERAPIST: What remains hardest to say today?

[long pause]

OWEN: That I don't know how much. I used to know what I was doing all day.

THERAPIST: We can leave that uncertainty visible. Let's make the medical call and confirm the ride now.

OWEN: Can you stay while I talk?

THERAPIST: Yes.

OWEN: All right. Just... give me a second.
