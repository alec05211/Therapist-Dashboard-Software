# Owen Park session 5 The promise his partner no longer trusts
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 4.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: How did the two planned calls go?

OWEN: One happened. Monday. Wednesday I watched the phone ring.

THERAPIST: What happened Monday?

OWEN: He told me about the school run. I walked to the library afterward. Didn't stay long. I wanted to tell you I did both days.

THERAPIST: What stopped you?

OWEN: Having to remember another version.

THERAPIST: And Wednesday?

OWEN: An interview got cancelled. I was already upset. I drank. Didn't drive. I didn't measure it properly, so the record has a gap.

THERAPIST: Any physical symptoms since then that need medical attention now?

OWEN: No, not now. I told the medical service about the evening at follow-up. I didn't try changing the plan on my own.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: Where does the specialist appointment stand?

OWEN: Tomorrow. They said someone can meet me in reception. My brother can come to the door. I haven't been assessed yet.

THERAPIST: How do you feel about going?

OWEN: Worse when people ask if I'm excited.

THERAPIST: What would be a more useful question?

OWEN: Whether I know how to get there.

THERAPIST: Do you?

OWEN: Yes. Same bus. Brother meets me outside at ten.

[pause]

OWEN: My partner asked if this means things will be normal again. I said yes. Then they said they didn't believe me.

THERAPIST: How did you respond?

OWEN: Said they weren't giving me a chance. Then didn't talk for the rest of dinner.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What do you wish you had said?

OWEN: I don't know. Nothing sounds true enough.

THERAPIST: What part is true?

OWEN: The appointment is tomorrow. I don't know what happens after it.

THERAPIST: Could you start there?

OWEN: Maybe. I keep wanting the appointment to pay for everything. Like I can put it on the table and we're even.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: What do you understand about what your partner needs?

OWEN: Sleep. They stay up if I'm late. They told me that months ago. I said they were anxious.

THERAPIST: Have they said they want you to share that here?

OWEN: No. This is me telling you what I remember them saying. They're not here to give their version.

THERAPIST: We'll preserve that distinction.

## Part 4

OWEN: There's something I need you to change from before.

THERAPIST: What is it?

OWEN: You said, I think, that the drinking changed after I lost work. It did get worse. But I was hiding it before that. Three months before the layoff, maybe longer.

THERAPIST: Thank you for correcting the timeline. What did it look like then?

OWEN: Staying out after work. Saying it was networking. Some of it was. Sometimes I was alone.

THERAPIST: So job loss may have worsened an existing pattern. It wasn't necessarily the beginning.

OWEN: Yes. I liked the story where it was just six bad weeks.

THERAPIST: What becomes harder with the longer timeline?

OWEN: Thinking about how many conversations I let my partner have with the wrong information.

[long pause]

OWEN: I don't want to count them today.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: We can stay with what is possible. Do you want to practice what you might say tonight?

OWEN: I have the appointment tomorrow. I'm planning to go. I don't know what they'll recommend.

THERAPIST: What about trust?

OWEN: I can't ask you to believe it all right now.

[pause]

OWEN: That sounds like something from a leaflet.

THERAPIST: How would you say it?

OWEN: You don't have to say it's okay. I just wanted you to know where I'll be tomorrow.

THERAPIST: Does that feel closer?

OWEN: Yes. I can say that much.

THERAPIST: Your partner may still be upset.

OWEN: I know. I don't know how to sit at dinner with that. I usually wash everything immediately so I can leave the room.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: What would be a small way to stay without forcing the conversation?

OWEN: Ask if they want company for ten minutes. Let them say no.

THERAPIST: Would that leave you with enough support afterward?

OWEN: I can call my brother at the time we agreed. My partner can't be the whole plan.

THERAPIST: Any change in driving access or thoughts of harming yourself?

OWEN: Keys are still with them and my brother. No driving after drinking. No thoughts of killing myself or hurting myself. I'm embarrassed, and I'm going home by bus.

THERAPIST: What should we check next time?

OWEN: Whether I actually went tomorrow. What they said, if I can bring it. And Wednesday. I don't want the library walk to erase Wednesday.

THERAPIST: We'll keep both.

OWEN: Okay. I think that's all I can do today.
