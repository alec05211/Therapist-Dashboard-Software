# Owen Park session 9 Repair without surveillance
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 8.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: How did the group orientation go?

OWEN: I went. Sat near the door. Didn't speak until the end.

THERAPIST: What did you say then?

OWEN: Asked when the evening group meets. I haven't committed to that group yet.

THERAPIST: What was useful enough to ask?

OWEN: Someone said support shouldn't require a family member to be on call. That landed.

THERAPIST: How did it land?

OWEN: Badly first. Then accurately.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: You wanted to talk about monitoring and privacy with your partner.

OWEN: They asked to see every receipt and my location history.

THERAPIST: What did you say?

OWEN: At first, "fine," because I wanted the argument over. Then I said I could share the care plan I agreed to, but not hand over my phone every night.

THERAPIST: How did your partner respond?

OWEN: Said they weren't trying to be my clinician. They were trying not to be surprised again.

[pause]

THERAPIST: What did you hear in that?

OWEN: The cost of what I hid. Also a limit we haven't solved.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What accountability did you both agree to?

OWEN: If I drink, I say so that day. If I feel unsafe to drive, I don't negotiate for keys. We have one planned check-in after dinner, not constant questions.

THERAPIST: Is that agreement in place now?

OWEN: We are trying it for a week. Either of us can say it isn't working.

THERAPIST: Who holds responsibility for clinical support?

OWEN: Me and the professionals. Not my partner. My brother isn't the night service either.

THERAPIST: Does your partner agree with that wording?

OWEN: With the idea. Maybe not my wording.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: How has alcohol use been since Tuesday?

OWEN: One evening. I told my partner that night and used the specialist call afterward.

THERAPIST: Did you drive or have acute symptoms?

OWEN: No driving. No current symptoms. The keys are still with my brother.

THERAPIST: What did the call change?

OWEN: It stopped the evening from becoming private. It did not make the drinking disappear.

THERAPIST: That distinction seems important.

OWEN: Yes. Earlier honesty is not the same as no problem.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: How is housing uncertainty now?

OWEN: We agreed not to make a move decision this week. That is not the same as deciding to stay together.

THERAPIST: And employment?

OWEN: Two applications, no interviews. The rejection still stings. I changed the calendar sound.

THERAPIST: Did changing the sound remove the five-o'clock cue?

OWEN: No. Five still happens. It just doesn't sound like my old laptop.

THERAPIST: What else happens at five now?

OWEN: On group days, I leave at five ten. On other days, that part is still thin.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Any thoughts of killing yourself or harming yourself this week?

OWEN: No. No plan or steps. I had one night where I wanted everyone to stop asking things of me.

THERAPIST: What helped you keep that from becoming isolation?

OWEN: The scheduled check-in. Because it had an end time.

THERAPIST: What do you want to test next?

OWEN: Attend the evening group once. Keep the one check-in. Ask the care team what would need to be true before the keys come back.

THERAPIST: Whose decision is that last one?

OWEN: A shared safety decision. Not a prize from my brother.

THERAPIST: That is clear.
