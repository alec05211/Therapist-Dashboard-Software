# Owen Park session 4 Five oclock without a job
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 3.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: How did the appointments go?

OWEN: Friday happened. Tuesday didn't.

THERAPIST: What happened on Tuesday?

OWEN: I got to the building. Sat outside. Went home.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: What was happening while you sat there?

OWEN: People going through the door. I kept thinking someone would recognize me. It's not even near my old office.

THERAPIST: Did the service know you were outside?

OWEN: No. I wrote that I had transport trouble. I had a bus pass.

THERAPIST: Would you like help contacting them with what actually happened?

OWEN: Maybe. I don't want another person being disappointed.

THERAPIST: We can ask what support is available for getting through the first appointment.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: What came out of the medical follow-up?

OWEN: I showed the list. We talked about sleep and alcohol. They gave me a follow-up plan. I said you could coordinate with them, but I haven't signed your form yet.

THERAPIST: We'll review the scope before any contact. We still shouldn't claim I have their findings.

OWEN: Okay.

THERAPIST: How are you physically today?

OWEN: Tired. No shaking or feeling sick right now. I know who to call if that changes. I haven't tried making up my own stopping plan.

THERAPIST: And driving?

OWEN: Still not after drinking. The keys are where we said.

THERAPIST: Has anyone been unavailable when you needed a ride?

OWEN: Once. I stayed home. It wasn't a good evening, but I stayed home.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What made the evening difficult?

OWEN: Five o'clock.

THERAPIST: Tell me what happens then.

OWEN: My laptop makes the calendar noise. I still have a reminder to send the team update. I haven't deleted it.

THERAPIST: Does that happen every day?

OWEN: Weekdays. By then I'm already looking at the clock.

THERAPIST: What do you notice yourself wanting?

OWEN: To have finished something. To be allowed to stop.

[long pause]

OWEN: I used to walk to the train at five. Call my brother. Now I go to the kitchen.

THERAPIST: Would calling him at that time be something you'd want to try again?

OWEN: If he isn't supposed to fix me.

THERAPIST: What could you ask for instead?

OWEN: Ten minutes. Tell me about his day.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: How many times would feel manageable to try that?

OWEN: Two. Monday and Wednesday. Before five, maybe. Once I'm in the kitchen I stop answering.

THERAPIST: Have you asked him yet?

OWEN: No. This is still an idea.

THERAPIST: We'll keep the record that way. What if he can't answer?

OWEN: Walk to the library. It closes at six. I used to go there without making it a project.

THERAPIST: Is there a safe way to get there without the car?

OWEN: Ten-minute walk. I could leave earlier.

THERAPIST: What might make that difficult?

OWEN: Seeing people. Some days I can buy groceries. Other days I can't get through the door. I don't know which I'll be.

THERAPIST: We can review what happens without turning two attempts into a verdict.

## Part 5

OWEN: My brother said he couldn't do every night. I hadn't asked every night. I heard it like he'd already had enough.

THERAPIST: What did he actually say?

OWEN: That his kid has bedtime and he wants to know when I'm calling.

THERAPIST: A limit on availability.

OWEN: Yes. It felt bigger.

THERAPIST: Can both of those stay in the story?

OWEN: I suppose.

[long pause]

OWEN: He sent another lunch picture afterward. So maybe he wasn't done with me.

THERAPIST: How did you respond?

OWEN: Told him the rice looked dry. He said it was. That's about the longest conversation I had yesterday.

THERAPIST: Did it help at all?

OWEN: For a few minutes. I was still sad afterward.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Before we finish, any change in thoughts about harming yourself or wanting to die?

OWEN: No. Still wanting the day over sometimes. No plan or steps or wanting to die.

THERAPIST: Do we need to change the contact plan or tonight's arrangements?

OWEN: No. My brother knows I'm calling after this. Can we call the assessment place before I go?

THERAPIST: Yes. What do you want them to know?

OWEN: That I got there. Didn't go in. And I want to try again with someone meeting me at the door, if they can.

THERAPIST: We'll ask. We shouldn't promise what they can provide.

OWEN: I know.

[long pause]

OWEN: I don't want to say I'm ready. I just want another appointment.

THERAPIST: That's enough to ask for.

OWEN: Okay. Let's do that before I change the subject.
