# Owen Park session 6 Craving and the long evening
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 5.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: Did you get through the appointment?

OWEN: Yes. My brother walked in with me. Stayed in reception when they called me through.

THERAPIST: What did you come away with?

OWEN: A follow-up appointment. They want more history before settling the plan. I signed their permission form to talk with you. I haven't brought yours back yet.

THERAPIST: We need to confirm the authorization before any exchange. I haven't spoken with them or received a diagnosis.

OWEN: I haven't got a diagnosis to give you. I mostly got through the questions.

THERAPIST: What was that like?

OWEN: They waited when I stopped. I thought they'd fill it in for me.

THERAPIST: Was that helpful?

OWEN: Uncomfortable. Helpful later, maybe. I haven't decided.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: Last time you wanted us to return to Wednesday as well as the library walk.

OWEN: Wednesday was bad. This week was better. Except Friday.

THERAPIST: What happened Friday?

OWEN: Actual interview this time. Not cancelled. They asked what I'd done since the layoff. I said consulting. I haven't been consulting.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: What happened after the interview?

OWEN: Went to the shop. Stood outside. Called my brother. He didn't answer.

THERAPIST: What did you do then?

OWEN: Walked to the library. Sat by the magazines. I didn't drink that evening.

THERAPIST: What helped you stay there?

OWEN: There were other people. Nobody needed me to explain myself. I texted him where I was.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What happened over the rest of the weekend?

OWEN: Quiet.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: Does quiet tell the whole story?

OWEN: No. Saturday I drank at home. I was going to stop at Friday because it sounds better.

THERAPIST: Do you know how much?

OWEN: More than I wrote at first. I corrected it before coming. I can show the medical clinician the record. I don't want us to invent a clean total where I didn't measure.

THERAPIST: Have you contacted the medical service about Saturday and any symptoms since?

OWEN: Yes. I told them. I'm following their directions. No shaking, confusion, or feeling sick now.

THERAPIST: Did you drive, or have access to the keys?

OWEN: No driving. The key arrangement hasn't changed.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: So Friday you used another place when your first support wasn't available. Saturday still needs attention.

OWEN: I thought you'd be annoyed I ruined the good part.

THERAPIST: They are both part of the week. What was different Saturday?

OWEN: My partner went to see a friend. I said I wanted space. Then I hated the space.

THERAPIST: Did you reach out to anyone?

OWEN: No. I didn't want to be another evening on someone's phone.

THERAPIST: Has your brother said that?

OWEN: No. He said he can't answer every time. That's different.

[pause]

OWEN: When he called back Friday, he was in a school meeting. He wasn't ignoring me.

THERAPIST: It sounds useful to keep his actual availability separate from what an unanswered call means to you.

OWEN: I can do that here. Harder at home.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: Did you talk with your partner as you planned?

OWEN: Told them about the appointment. Asked if they wanted company. They said not that night.

THERAPIST: How did you manage that?

OWEN: Went to the other room. Didn't send the long text I wrote. That doesn't mean things are repaired.

THERAPIST: What would you want the record to say?

OWEN: They said no. I let the no stay. We're still figuring out whether living together works.

THERAPIST: Is your housing changing now?

OWEN: No decision. I'm scared it might. Please don't write that I've moved out.

THERAPIST: I'll record it as uncertainty you've described.

OWEN: And I'm not getting a job offer. The interview is still just an interview.

THERAPIST: We'll keep that separate too.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Any thoughts of hurting yourself or wanting to die since Saturday?

OWEN: Wanting the evening gone, yes. Wanting to die, no. No plan or steps to harm myself. I can call my brother tonight. We confirmed a time.

THERAPIST: Let's review the urgent support plan if that changes, and the medical contact plan. Then what would help with evenings when he can't answer?

OWEN: Ask the service about a support that isn't my family. Keep the library as one option while it's open. I need something for later too.

THERAPIST: That's an open question for us and the specialist to work on. We don't have to pretend it's already arranged.

[long pause]

OWEN: I used to leave out the bad part until next week. I said Saturday today.

THERAPIST: What is it like to notice that?

OWEN: Still bad. A little less lonely. Can we stop there?

THERAPIST: Yes. We'll check the practical plans before you leave.
