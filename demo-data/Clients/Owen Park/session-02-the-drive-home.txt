# Owen Park session 2 The drive home
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 1.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: Where would you like to begin?

OWEN: I made the call.

THERAPIST: The medical service we called together last time?

OWEN: Yes. The nurse spoke to me that afternoon. I went to the appointment they arranged. My brother took me.

THERAPIST: How did that go?

OWEN: A lot of questions. I have written instructions. They want another appointment with the substance-use service. I haven't booked that part.

THERAPIST: Would you be willing to bring the instructions, and consider permission for us to coordinate?

OWEN: Bring them, yes. Permission... I want to read what that means first.

THERAPIST: We can do that. For now I have your report of the visit, not the medical record.

OWEN: Right. I didn't bring proof.

THERAPIST: We can be precise without treating you like you're on trial.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: What happened with the car keys?

OWEN: My partner has the spare.

THERAPIST: Where is your set?

[long pause]

OWEN: In my coat.

THERAPIST: So you still have access.

OWEN: Yes.

THERAPIST: Have you driven after drinking since we met?

OWEN: No. My brother's been driving. You can ask him.

THERAPIST: That's your report, and it's important. We also need a plan for when he isn't available.

OWEN: I can give over my keys too.

THERAPIST: Who could hold them, by agreement?

OWEN: My brother. My partner said they don't want to be the police.

THERAPIST: Would your brother agree to that responsibility?

OWEN: I'll ask. I shouldn't say he's agreed because I want to be done talking about it.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: Can we return to the drive itself?

OWEN: There's not much to it.

THERAPIST: What do you remember?

OWEN: Leaving the restaurant. The turn onto our street. Sitting outside before I went in.

THERAPIST: Was anyone else in the car?

OWEN: No.

[long pause]

OWEN: My partner was supposed to be. They saw me and called a ride instead.

THERAPIST: Last time you described it as a short trip. How far was it?

OWEN: Maybe twenty minutes. I was thinking of the last part near the house.

THERAPIST: We'll correct the earlier description. No collision, no passenger, and a longer drive than you first described.

OWEN: I didn't forget the length. I wanted it smaller.

THERAPIST: What happens for you saying that here?

OWEN: I want to leave.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: Do you need us to slow down?

OWEN: Just don't ask me what I learned from it yet.

THERAPIST: All right.

[long pause]

OWEN: They stood by the passenger door. I remember not looking over.

THERAPIST: Your partner?

OWEN: Yes. They weren't yelling. I keep telling it like there was an argument. There wasn't.

THERAPIST: What would you like me to understand about that?

OWEN: I could have stopped. They'd already called the ride. There was a seat.

THERAPIST: You're describing an alternative that was available then. What could make an alternative easier to use next time, before drinking begins?

OWEN: Not taking the car. Booking the ride out as well as back.

THERAPIST: That sounds concrete. How would that work this weekend?

OWEN: I can go with my brother. No car with me.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: How has the week been physically?

OWEN: Tired. No shaking today. I'm following the instructions they gave me. I don't want to guess at what the clinician called it.

THERAPIST: We can review the document when you bring it. Any new symptoms or concerns they need to hear about?

OWEN: Sleep. Still bad. I haven't told them how late I stay awake.

THERAPIST: Let's add that to the follow-up questions. Any thoughts of harming yourself since last time?

OWEN: No. I feel ashamed. That's different for me, though I know you have to ask.

THERAPIST: I do want to ask rather than assume. What has helped you stay connected this week?

OWEN: My brother sends pictures of his terrible lunches. I answer those. Most other things I leave unread.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Would you like to ask him about the keys now?

OWEN: I'll text. [pause] He says yes. He's picking me up here. He can take them then.

THERAPIST: Until then, what is the plan?

OWEN: I stay here and wait. I'm not driving today.

THERAPIST: And if the arrangement becomes unavailable or you feel unable to keep the no-driving plan?

OWEN: Call for help before getting near the car. Arrange another ride. Tell someone instead of just going quiet.

THERAPIST: We'll check what happened next time. Anything you want corrected in how I've understood today?

OWEN: My partner isn't doing this with me. They're deciding what they can live with. That's not the same as supporting every part of my plan.

THERAPIST: I'll keep that distinction.

OWEN: Thanks. I don't really have anything else.
