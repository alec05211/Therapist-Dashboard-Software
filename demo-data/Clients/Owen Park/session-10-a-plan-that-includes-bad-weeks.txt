# Owen Park session 10 A plan that includes bad weeks
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 9.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: You brought the care-plan page. What feels most important on it?

OWEN: The part that says a hard week changes the support, not whether I deserve support.

THERAPIST: Is that your wording or the team's?

OWEN: Mine, after they gave me a more official sentence.

THERAPIST: How was the evening group?

OWEN: I attended once. I listened. I said my name and that evenings are difficult.

THERAPIST: Do you plan to return?

OWEN: Next week, yes. I am not promising forever.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: What has a bad week meant recently?

OWEN: Two drinking evenings, missing a planned walk, and not answering my partner's question until morning.

THERAPIST: What happened with driving and the keys?

OWEN: No driving. The keys stayed with my brother. I used rides and the bus.

THERAPIST: Any acute symptoms or change in medical guidance?

OWEN: No current symptoms. I updated the service and followed their guidance. I don't want to restate medical details here.

THERAPIST: We can keep that boundary.

OWEN: The old version of me would call this proof the whole plan failed.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What does the current version call it?

OWEN: A bad week. Not a secret week.

THERAPIST: What made it less secret?

OWEN: I told the group contact and my partner. I wrote the amounts I knew. I didn't improve the story before the appointment.

THERAPIST: What remained hard?

OWEN: My partner looked tired, not proud. I wanted earlier honesty to earn a better reaction.

THERAPIST: Did you tell them that?

OWEN: No. I noticed it here first.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: What structure exists now that did not exist at session one?

OWEN: Medical follow-up. Specialist check-ins. One group I might keep attending. My brother has the keys. The library and map exhibit are daytime options.

THERAPIST: What gaps remain?

OWEN: Late evenings. Job rejection days. The part where I decide support is for someone worse.

THERAPIST: And the relationship?

OWEN: Less constant checking this week. Still not repaired. No housing decision.

THERAPIST: Employment?

OWEN: Still looking. No offer.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: How do you define progress right now?

OWEN: Earlier honesty. Safer action before I feel convinced. Letting a bad night change tomorrow's plan.

THERAPIST: What does progress not mean?

OWEN: A flawless story. Or that reduced use equals no risk.

THERAPIST: Is there anything you miss from the first plan?

OWEN: Privacy. But some of what I called privacy was hiding.

THERAPIST: Are they always the same?

OWEN: No. Session nine was trying to separate them.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Any thoughts of killing yourself or harming yourself during the bad week?

OWEN: No thoughts of killing myself, no plan, no steps. I did want to sleep through the next day.

THERAPIST: Did you use the safety plan?

OWEN: I told the specialist contact and didn't stay alone with it. We reviewed what would require urgent help.

THERAPIST: What is the next step rather than the final answer?

OWEN: Go back to group. Keep the check-ins bounded. Ask about later-evening support. Review keys with the team, not because I am impatient.

THERAPIST: And here?

OWEN: Keep talking about who I am when there isn't a job title. Slowly.

THERAPIST: Slowly is allowed.
