# Owen Park session 12 A quieter measure of progress
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. Two weeks after session 11.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: It has been two weeks. How would you describe them without summarizing too quickly?

OWEN: Uneven. Three group meetings, one missed. Two drinking evenings. More calls before five than after.

THERAPIST: Any driving or change in key access?

OWEN: No driving. The team agreed I can start a written transportation review, but my brother still has the keys today.

THERAPIST: So review has begun; access has not changed.

OWEN: Correct. Please keep those separate.

THERAPIST: Any current acute symptoms?

OWEN: No. Medical follow-up continues.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: What happened on the drinking evenings?

OWEN: One was after an argument. I called afterward, not before. The other was a Saturday with no obvious crisis.

THERAPIST: Does the second one fit the five-o'clock explanation?

OWEN: Not neatly. It was earlier and I had plans. I don't want the calendar cue to become the answer to everything.

THERAPIST: What might that discrepancy ask us to review?

OWEN: Habit. Availability. Maybe wanting not to account for every hour. I don't know yet.

THERAPIST: We can keep multiple possibilities open.

OWEN: Good. One pattern shouldn't eat the whole record.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: How is the agreement with your partner working?

OWEN: We kept the one check-in most nights. They asked for my location twice; I said no once and shared it once because I wanted to.

THERAPIST: What made those different?

OWEN: Consent, I think. And whether it was about immediate safety or making anxiety go away.

THERAPIST: Is the relationship repaired?

OWEN: No. It is less chaotic. We agreed to remain in the apartment through next month and revisit it. That is not a permanent decision.

THERAPIST: How did it feel to make a time-limited decision?

OWEN: Less dramatic. Also sad.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: What happened with work?

OWEN: I accepted a short contract screening project. Six weeks, part time, starts Monday.

THERAPIST: Is that the same as returning to your old role?

OWEN: No. No benefits, no promise after six weeks, and I work from home.

THERAPIST: What risks and supports do you see?

OWEN: Structure could help. The laptop and five o'clock could also get louder. I told the care team before saying yes.

THERAPIST: What boundary will you test?

OWEN: Close the laptop at five, leave the apartment, and not call overtime proof that I am useful.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: At session one, what did progress mean to you?

OWEN: Proving I could control it privately and getting everyone to stop worrying.

THERAPIST: What does it mean now?

OWEN: Telling the truth sooner. Not driving. Letting professionals adjust the plan. Keeping my partner and brother out of jobs they can't do.

THERAPIST: What has not resolved?

OWEN: I still drink. Shame still makes me edit the first version. Evenings are still hard, including ones without a rejection.

THERAPIST: And what is different?

OWEN: Sometimes I correct the first version before someone catches it.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Any thoughts of killing yourself or harming yourself in the last two weeks?

OWEN: No thoughts of killing myself, no plan, no steps. One night I wanted to skip ahead to morning. I used the plan and called.

THERAPIST: What should the next phase keep in view?

OWEN: The contract start, the transportation review, later evenings, and whether group remains useful. Also the apartment decision next month.

THERAPIST: What should it avoid claiming?

OWEN: That work fixed me. That the relationship is repaired. That starting a key review means I have the keys. Or that telling the truth makes drinking safe.

THERAPIST: That is a precise set of boundaries.

[long pause]

OWEN: It feels quieter than progress is supposed to feel.

THERAPIST: Quieter can still be observable.

OWEN: We can stop there.
