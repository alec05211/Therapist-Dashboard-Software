# Owen Park session 11 The call before five
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 10.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: What happened with the second-round interview?

OWEN: They chose someone else. The email came at four twenty.

THERAPIST: What did you notice first?

OWEN: I started drafting the polite reply. Then I looked at the clock.

THERAPIST: What did the clock mean?

OWEN: That five was coming and I was already bargaining with myself.

THERAPIST: What did you do?

OWEN: Called the group contact at four thirty-five. Before the calendar sound.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: Was the contact available?

OWEN: Yes, for ten minutes. They didn't talk me out of feeling bad.

THERAPIST: What was useful?

OWEN: We named the next hour. I walked to the library and stayed until close.

THERAPIST: Did you drink that evening?

[long pause]

OWEN: Later. One can at home. I told my partner before bed.

THERAPIST: So calling earlier changed part of the evening, not all of it.

OWEN: Right. I don't want it recorded as either a complete success or a failure.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: Any driving or attempt to get the keys?

OWEN: No driving. I used the bus. The keys are still with my brother.

THERAPIST: Any current physical symptoms or need for urgent medical care?

OWEN: No current symptoms. I updated the service the next morning as planned.

THERAPIST: What did your partner say when you told them?

OWEN: "Thank you for telling me. I still hate this." Both sentences.

THERAPIST: How did you respond?

OWEN: I said both could stay. I didn't ask if they trusted me now.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: You also planned to take a radio out of the box.

OWEN: I did. It is missing a dial, not a screw. I had remembered it wrong.

THERAPIST: What was it like to look?

OWEN: Annoying. Familiar. I found the model number and ordered nothing.

THERAPIST: Why nothing?

OWEN: I wanted one evening where the task could remain unfinished without becoming evidence against me.

[pause]

THERAPIST: Did that happen?

OWEN: For about twenty minutes. Then I laughed at how serious I was being about a dial.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: What is changing in the five-o'clock pattern?

OWEN: I notice it sooner on some days. I have two places to go and one person outside my family to call.

THERAPIST: What is not changing enough yet?

OWEN: The hours after those places close. And the thought that one rejection means the whole day was fake.

THERAPIST: What about employment plans?

OWEN: I am applying to fewer jobs with better fit. That is a strategy, not a sign I have a job.

THERAPIST: Relationship and housing?

OWEN: Still living together. Still deciding. The nightly check-in stayed at one, not all evening.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: Any thoughts of killing yourself or harming yourself after the rejection?

OWEN: No. No plan or steps. I wanted to turn my phone off and be unreachable.

THERAPIST: What kept you reachable?

OWEN: The call was scheduled for ten minutes. I could tolerate ten minutes.

THERAPIST: What do you want to carry forward?

OWEN: Call before five when I can, not after I have decided the night is lost. Keep the later-evening gap visible.

THERAPIST: Anything else?

OWEN: Fix the radio eventually. Not as a metaphor.

THERAPIST: We can leave it as a radio.
