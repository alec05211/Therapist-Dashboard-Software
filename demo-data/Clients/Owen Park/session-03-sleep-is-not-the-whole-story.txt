# Owen Park session 3 Sleep is not the whole story
# Fictional dialogue draft for evaluation. One week after session 2.
# OWEN: Orus. THERAPIST: Jeremy Heartwell, Sadaltager. Directions are not spoken.

## Part 1

THERAPIST: How was getting here today?

OWEN: Bus. I missed the first one. I almost went home.

THERAPIST: What brought you through the door?

OWEN: I had the paper you asked for.

THERAPIST: Thank you for bringing it. Before we look, what would you like us to understand about the week?

OWEN: I booked the assessment. Tuesday next week. The medical follow-up is Friday. Two different places.

THERAPIST: So the specialist assessment hasn't happened yet.

OWEN: No. The first appointment was medical. I think I said it like I'd done everything.

THERAPIST: We'll keep those separate.

OWEN: Can you not call it compliance? I saw that word on a form.

THERAPIST: We can describe what you've done and what's still pending.

## Part 2

THERAPIST: With your permission, let's review these instructions together.

OWEN: Okay. I marked the number they said to call if things change. I haven't called since the visit.

THERAPIST: How have things changed physically?

OWEN: No shaking today. Still awake until three sometimes. Last night I slept five hours. Woke up feeling like I hadn't.

THERAPIST: What have you been taking or drinking around bedtime?

OWEN: I put the sleep product away after the medical visit. I brought a picture of the label for Friday. Alcohol's still part of the evening. I wrote it down for them. I don't want to read the list out loud today.

THERAPIST: We need enough information to address current safety. We can discuss how to share the rest with the medical clinician. Are you acutely unwell now?

OWEN: No.

## Part 3

THERAPIST: What did drinking used to do for sleep?

OWEN: Made me stop waiting for it.

THERAPIST: And now?

OWEN: I fall asleep, then I'm awake again. I tell people I slept because technically I did.

[long pause]

THERAPIST: What fills the time when you wake?

OWEN: Old messages. From work. I read the ones where people needed me to answer something.

THERAPIST: What is that like?

OWEN: Worse afterward.

THERAPIST: Would you like to say more?

OWEN: No. Sorry.

THERAPIST: You don't need to apologize.

[long pause]

OWEN: I don't miss recruiting that much. I miss having a reason someone would call.

THERAPIST: That sounds important. We don't have to resolve it today.

OWEN: Please don't make it into a breakthrough. I still check the messages.

## Part 4

THERAPIST: Last time we talked about your brother holding the keys. What happened?

OWEN: He took my set when he picked me up. My partner still has the spare. I haven't driven after drinking since the restaurant.

THERAPIST: That's what you're reporting today. How is the arrangement working?

OWEN: Annoying. Better than bargaining about whether I can go somewhere.

THERAPIST: Have you had moments of wanting the keys back?

OWEN: Saturday. I wanted food. My brother ordered it with me on the phone. I was angry about that for no good reason.

THERAPIST: What was the anger about?

OWEN: Needing permission. Even though I asked for the arrangement.

THERAPIST: Does it feel like permission from him, or a reminder of why you made the agreement?

OWEN: Both. I don't have a better answer.

## Part 5

THERAPIST: You sound quieter today. What would you want me to understand about that?

OWEN: I'm tired. I'm also embarrassed that you remember things I said.

THERAPIST: Would it help to check them together rather than assume they're still true?

OWEN: Yes. Especially the part about my partner helping. They're home, but we aren't really talking.

THERAPIST: Being in the same home doesn't tell us how available that support is.

OWEN: Right.

THERAPIST: Have you had thoughts of dying, or hurting yourself, this week?

OWEN: Not of doing anything. I sometimes wish I could miss a whole day. I mean sleep through it. I haven't wanted to be dead.

THERAPIST: Any plan, intention, or steps to harm yourself?

OWEN: No.

## Part 6

THERAPIST: If the wish to get away changes into thoughts of harming yourself, who could you tell directly?

OWEN: My brother. And get urgent help if I can't stay safe. I have the contact plan we wrote down.

THERAPIST: Let's check that the numbers and available supports are still right before you leave. What else would be useful today?

OWEN: I want Friday to be less awful. Could we write down the things I keep leaving out?

THERAPIST: Yes. Sleep, the product label, the drinking record, and the questions you want answered. Anything else?

[long pause]

OWEN: That I avoid the appointment because I'm scared. Not because I forgot.

THERAPIST: Would you like that on the list too?

OWEN: Maybe at the bottom. I could point to it.

THERAPIST: We can leave room for that.

OWEN: Okay. That would help.
