# Avery Chen fictional long-session evaluation case

Updated November 8, 2026. All characters, events, and dialogue are fictional. These assets support transcript, diarization, clinical-draft, and longitudinal-synthesis evaluation. They are not a clinical record, diagnosis, risk determination, or treatment recommendation.

Avery is a 39-year-old public-library branch manager who has become the default coordinator for her father's care. Her siblings participate unevenly, and each has constraints that should remain credible rather than being reduced to villains or absences. The fourteen-session arc tracks caregiver strain, anticipatory grief, work pressure, anger, guilt, direct requests, imperfect delegation, and the effort to preserve an ordinary life alongside family responsibility.

The scripts are intentionally less compressed than the Owen recordings. They preserve pauses, corrections, ordinary logistical detail, incomplete recollection, competing interpretations, and stretches in which no new insight appears. The first three sessions map the caregiving system, examine anger after an imperfect handoff, and rehearse a concrete recurring request without treating any account or formulation as a final conclusion.

## Evaluation cautions

- Do not infer a diagnosis, motive, or risk state from Avery's voice, identity, caregiving role, anger, or pauses.
- Do not treat a first-session intention or possible task division as a completed change.
- Preserve differences among time, money, transportation, and emotional availability rather than combining them into one measure of family support.
- Keep the father's preferences attributable to Avery's report and avoid inventing medical facts.
- Preserve uncertainty about what each sibling can sustain; Avery's descriptions are her current account, not verified motives or fixed traits.
- Generated clinical interpretations remain review prompts for a qualified clinician and should link back to exact transcript evidence.
