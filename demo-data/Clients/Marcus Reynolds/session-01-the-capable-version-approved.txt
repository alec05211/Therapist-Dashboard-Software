## Part 1 - Arriving

Jeremy: Before we begin, I want you to know that we can slow down, change direction, or leave something for another day. You do not have to make this a successful first session for me.

Marcus: [pause] Okay. I appreciate that. I think I have been trying to figure out what a successful version of this would even look like.

Jeremy: What have you imagined?

Marcus: That I would come in with a clear explanation. A beginning, a middle... some reason all of it fits together. [pause] I do not have that.

Jeremy: You do not need it.

Marcus: I know. Or... I know that when I am saying it to somebody else. I am a high-school guidance counselor. I ask students to sit in rooms like this and say things they do not have words for yet.

Jeremy: It feels different from this chair.

Marcus: Very different. [long pause] I almost canceled this morning.

Jeremy: What stopped you?

Marcus: I was tired of hearing myself say I would deal with it later. And there was this moment a couple weeks ago. At a grocery store. I can tell you about that. I just... maybe not first.

Jeremy: We can come back to it. Where would it feel easier to start?

Marcus: Sleep. That feels factual. I wake up around three. Three-fifteen if it is a good night. Then my mind starts going through everything I might have missed.

Jeremy: What kinds of things?

Marcus: Work. My father. My brother and sister. Bills. Messages I have not answered. Things I said during the day. Things I should have said. [pause] It is like I wake up already behind.

Jeremy: And then?

Marcus: I lie there and try to make myself stop thinking. That usually makes it worse. By five or five-thirty, I get up.

Jeremy: How long has that been happening?

Marcus: On and off for almost a year. More often lately. I kept telling myself it would pass when work settled down, but work settles and I still wake up... braced. That is the word. Braced for something.

Jeremy: What is it like to say that here?

Marcus: [pause] It makes it sound more serious than I want it to be.

Jeremy: Do you want it to be less serious, or do you want me to see it as less serious?

Marcus: [long pause] I am not sure there is a difference yet.

## Part 2 - Functioning

Jeremy: You used the word braced. What happens once the day begins?

Marcus: I function. That is the word everybody uses. I shower. I get to work. I meet with students. I answer email. From the outside, I probably look the same.

Jeremy: And from the inside?

Marcus: [pause] Thin. Like there is not much between me and whatever happens next.

Jeremy: Can you give me an example?

Marcus: A colleague asked me about a spreadsheet last week. A normal question. I answered her like she had accused me of something. My voice got sharp before I even... before I understood I was angry.

Jeremy: What did you notice afterward?

Marcus: Her face. She looked surprised. Not hurt exactly. Maybe both. I apologized right away. She accepted it.

Jeremy: But it stayed with you.

Marcus: For three nights. [pause] I kept replaying it and thinking, this is who you are becoming. Somebody people have to be careful around.

Jeremy: That sounds frightening.

Marcus: It is. I work with teenagers. They notice everything. I cannot be... unpredictable with them.

Jeremy: Has that happened with a student?

Marcus: No. No, I do not think so. [pause] I hear myself answering too quickly. I have been less patient. I have not said anything cruel, but sometimes I am listening and part of me is just waiting for them to stop talking.

Jeremy: And that is not how you want to feel with them.

Marcus: It is not how I used to feel. I care about them. I still care. That is what scares me. The care is there, but it is buried under... I do not know. Noise. Exhaustion.

Jeremy: What happens at the end of the workday?

Marcus: I sit in my car. Ten minutes. Sometimes twenty. I know I could go inside, but opening the door feels like one more instruction I have to follow.

Jeremy: Are you resting in the car?

Marcus: Not really. I am suspended. Then I go upstairs, heat up something, and put on the television. I usually cannot tell you what I watched.

Jeremy: What is your mind doing?

Marcus: Reviewing. Looking for what I missed. Whether my father sounded tired on the phone. Whether my sister needed something she did not ask for. Whether a student is safe at home. [pause] I know thinking harder does not protect any of them. I cannot seem to stop.

Jeremy: What do you tell yourself about that?

Marcus: That I should know better. I tell students that struggling does not mean they have failed. Then I go home and treat my own exhaustion like evidence against me.

Jeremy: Does it feel like evidence against you here?

Marcus: [long pause] Less than I expected. But I am still trying to make it sound manageable.

## Part 3 - What changed

Jeremy: You said this began around a year ago. What was happening then?

Marcus: My mother died eleven months ago. Complications from an autoimmune illness. She had been sick for a while, but the end was fast. Faster than any of us understood while it was happening.

Jeremy: I am sorry, Marcus.

Marcus: Thank you. [long pause] I always say that part the same way. Eleven months. Complications. Brief hospitalization. The words are accurate, but they keep me far away from what happened.

Jeremy: What would be closer to it?

Marcus: [pause] I do not know. The phone ringing before sunrise. My sister trying to tell me something and not being able to finish the sentence. My father sitting in a chair with his coat still on.

Jeremy: You can take your time.

Marcus: I thought we had more time. Everybody says that, I know, but... I really thought we had more time.

Jeremy: What had the months before that been like?

Marcus: Driving to Baltimore. Appointments. Medications. Insurance calls. Making sure my father understood what the doctor said. My brother and sister helped. I was the one with the master list.

Jeremy: Was that a role you chose?

Marcus: Yes. And no. I am the oldest. I am good at organizing things. [pause] Doing something felt better than waiting. If I could make the next call, then I did not have to think about what the call meant.

Jeremy: Action gave you somewhere to put the fear.

Marcus: Maybe. [long pause] Yes. I think that is true. I do not like how true it feels.

Jeremy: What do you not like about it?

Marcus: It makes me wonder if I was present with her. I was always doing the next thing. Checking a number. Asking a nurse a question. I keep thinking... did she need me to stop? Did she need me to just sit there?

Jeremy: Do you have a memory of sitting with her?

Marcus: [pause] Her kitchen. A few months before the hospital. She was at the table, and there was music playing from this old radio she refused to replace. I was washing dishes. She told me to leave them alone.

Jeremy: Did you?

Marcus: No. [long pause] I told her I was almost done.

Jeremy: What comes up as you remember that?

Marcus: I miss her. And I feel... ashamed that even in the good memory I could not sit down.

Jeremy: Could the memory hold both your care and the difficulty stopping, without deciding what it says about you yet?

Marcus: I want to say yes. [pause] I am not there yet.

Jeremy: That is okay.

## Part 4 - The grocery store

Jeremy: Earlier you mentioned something that happened at a grocery store. Do you want to return to it?

Marcus: [pause] Yes. Two weeks ago. I was standing near the cereal aisle. Nothing unusual was happening. I was looking at a shelf and suddenly I could not get a full breath.

Jeremy: What else did you notice?

Marcus: My heart was racing. My hands went cold. The sounds got strange. Too close and far away at the same time. I remember looking at my basket and not understanding what I was supposed to do with it.

Jeremy: What did you think was happening?

Marcus: At first I thought something was wrong with my heart. Then I tried to reason myself out of it, but every thought made the fear sharper. I could not trust what my body was telling me.

Jeremy: What did you do?

Marcus: I left the basket and walked outside. I leaned against the building. [pause] Somebody asked if I was okay.

Jeremy: Were you able to answer?

Marcus: I said yes. Immediately. I could barely breathe, and I said yes like she had asked whether I wanted a receipt.

Jeremy: What was it like to be seen in that moment?

Marcus: Awful. Exposed. She was kind. That almost made it worse.

Jeremy: Because kindness meant you might have to receive something?

Marcus: Maybe. [long pause] Yes. If she had ignored me, I could have kept pretending it was private.

Jeremy: How long did it last?

Marcus: The worst of it was maybe ten minutes. I called my friend Andre. I told him I felt strange. I did not tell him everything. He stayed on the phone until I got home.

Jeremy: Have there been other episodes?

Marcus: Smaller ones. A wave in my chest. My vision narrowing for a second. I scheduled a physical. The doctor did not find anything urgent and suggested I look at stress and sleep.

Jeremy: I am glad you had it medically evaluated. What feels most troubling about it now?

Marcus: That my body did something I could not manage. [pause] And that my first instinct was still to make sure nobody else had to deal with it.

Jeremy: Being the person who needs something feels different from being the person who gives it.

Marcus: Very different. If I am useful, I know where I stand. If I need something... I do not know what I owe afterward.

Jeremy: Is care something that creates a debt?

Marcus: I would tell anyone else no. [long pause] For me, it feels more complicated.

## Part 5 - Who takes care of Marcus

Jeremy: Who takes care of you, Marcus?

Marcus: [long pause] I knew you might ask me something like that. I still do not know how to answer it.

Jeremy: We do not have to force an answer. What comes up when you hear the question?

Marcus: I have people. Andre. My friend Simone. My siblings. My father, in his way.

Jeremy: And who knows how this year has actually felt for you?

Marcus: [long pause] I do not know.

Jeremy: Take your time.

Marcus: Andre knows I am tired. Simone knows I miss my mother. My sister knows I worry about Dad. They each have a piece. I do not think anybody has the whole thing.

Jeremy: What might happen if one person had more of it?

Marcus: They would worry. They would check on me. [pause] They might start treating me like I am fragile.

Jeremy: What would be difficult about being checked on?

Marcus: I would have to answer honestly or lie. [long pause] That sounded worse out loud.

Jeremy: It sounded like a real dilemma.

Marcus: I am good at giving the version that is true enough. Work is busy. Sleep is strange. I am hanging in. Nobody can accuse me of lying, and nobody has to rearrange their day.

Jeremy: Does that version leave you feeling known?

Marcus: No. [pause] But it leaves me feeling competent.

Jeremy: Competent has been important.

Marcus: It has kept a lot of things moving. I do not want to turn it into a flaw. My mother trusted me. My students trust me. I like being dependable.

Jeremy: I do not hear it as a flaw. I wonder whether a strength becomes expensive when it is the only option you allow yourself.

Marcus: [long pause] That feels fair. I would like dependable to be something I choose, not something I have to prove every hour.

Jeremy: What do you notice after saying that?

Marcus: Relief. Then fear. If I stop carrying all of it, I do not know what gets dropped or who gets hurt.

Jeremy: We do not have to settle that fear today.

Marcus: Part of me wants you to. [pause] I know you cannot. I think I am used to conversations ending with a plan.

Jeremy: What is it like if this one does not resolve yet?

Marcus: Uncomfortable. [long pause] Maybe honest.

## Part 6 - A small experiment

Jeremy: As we come toward the end, what feels most important from this conversation?

Marcus: The difference between functioning and being okay. I knew there was a difference. I do not think I have been willing to apply it to myself.

Jeremy: What would applying it look like this week, in a very small way?

Marcus: My instinct is to turn this into another plan and grade myself on whether I follow it.

Jeremy: Even the solution starts to sound like more work.

Marcus: Yes. [pause] I do not want sleep to become another thing I fail at.

Jeremy: Then perhaps the goal is not to fix sleep this week.

Marcus: That sounds obvious when you say it. [long pause] It does not feel obvious at three in the morning.

Jeremy: What might feel possible at three in the morning?

Marcus: Write down the one thing I am afraid I will forget. Not the whole list. Just one thing. Then... maybe put my feet on the floor and notice where I am.

Jeremy: Does that feel like an experiment, or a requirement?

Marcus: An experiment. Once or twice. Not every night. [pause] I need to say that clearly or I will turn it into a rule before I reach the parking lot.

Jeremy: Once or twice. No score attached.

Marcus: Right.

Jeremy: Is there anything that would make the week slightly less solitary?

Marcus: I could tell Andre the real grocery-store story. Not the edited version where I felt off and went home.

Jeremy: What makes you hesitate?

Marcus: He will worry. And then he will know I did not tell him when it happened. [pause] I do not want him to think I do not trust him.

Jeremy: Is that why you did not tell him?

Marcus: No. I trust him. I did not want to become something he had to manage.

Jeremy: What could you ask from him that is smaller than being managed?

Marcus: I could say, can I tell you something without you trying to fix it? [long pause] He would understand that.

Jeremy: How likely are you to send that message?

Marcus: Maybe sixty percent.

Jeremy: What would make it sixty-five?

Marcus: Sending the first sentence before I leave here. Then waiting ten minutes before I send another message saying it is no big deal.

Jeremy: That sounds specific. How was it to talk here today?

Marcus: Harder than I expected. When you asked who takes care of me, I wanted to move away from the question. I still do.

Jeremy: We can return to it without demanding an answer before you are ready.

Marcus: [long pause] I do not feel better exactly. I feel... less alone with it than I did when I walked in.

Jeremy: Is that enough for today?

Marcus: Yes. [pause] I think I want there to be a next time.

Jeremy: We can start there.
